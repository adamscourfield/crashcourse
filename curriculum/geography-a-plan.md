## Course structure and choices

Paper1 Physical Environment:
- Changing landscapes of the UK: common geological/landscape overview; choose two of coasts, rivers and glaciated uplands.
- Weather hazards and climate change: circulation/heat, natural and human climate change, UK climate, tropical cyclones and drought, comparing impacts/responses across development contexts.
- Ecosystems, biodiversity and management: global biomes/biosphere resources, UK ecosystems, rainforest and deciduous woodland processes, threats and management.

Paper2 Human Environment:
- Changing cities: global urban/economic patterns; in-depth major UK city and developing/emerging-country city, including their context, growth, connections, challenges and responses.
- Global development: measures, unevenness, quality of life, strategies and an in-depth developing/emerging country’s development and sustainability.
- Resource management: common resource concepts, then choose energy or water. Build both option libraries.

Paper3 Investigations:
- Physical fieldwork: choose rivers or coasts; human fieldwork: choose central/inner urban area or rural settlement. Build all four preparations and the six common enquiry stages.
- UK challenges: resource consumption/environmental sustainability; settlement/population/economy; landscapes/flood risk; climate change. Require evidence-based synoptic judgement across topics, not memorised single-answer policies.

The specification requires three in-depth case studies (UK city, developing/emerging city, developing/emerging country) plus located examples in the content. Case-study library choices and data/source years must be briefed and verified before those lessons are built; do not assume everyone’s school uses one named set. Mark examples separately from required concepts so new located cases can reuse the learning engine. Check each topic’s required developed/emerging/developing comparisons.

## First batch and model design

Five shared introductory lessons: rock families; reading UK geology; geology/relief; physical landscape processes; people changing landscapes.15original practice questions and20 independent selected checks. Rock controls compare6 named samples; locator shows3 selected examples, not geological boundaries; profile swaps relative resistance under equal starting height/time; process sequence loops through past ice, post-glacial rivers and slopes/weathering; land-use layers compare farming/forestry/settlement.

BGS rocks/minerals, relief and geological dataset pages checked23October2026, plus South Downs National Park geological information for chalk. Links:

These are original schematic SVGs with labels, not official OS/BGS maps or calibrated models. Numerical erosion values are invented to support a controlled comparison. No model supplies an exact UK landform forecast. Process views default to looping playback, reduced-motion starts paused and manual stages remain available; direct specimen/map/evidence comparisons stay static. Keep the automatic answer flow and shared My progress summaries. Wrong answers and incomplete observation sets must not award completion.

## Fieldwork and assessment boundaries

Issue5 Appendix1 says at least two fieldwork **occasions**, outside classroom and school grounds; do not replace this wording with an unsupported claim of exactly two days. Physical/human contexts, qualitative/quantitative methods and required secondary sources are explicit in the fieldwork register. Simulations prepare for school fieldwork; they never certify participation. Extended8/12-mark writing, arguments, map construction, data handling and method marks need their own independent evidence and specialist assessment. Topic assessment is not yet built; each current lesson has an independent Check. Technical QA does not mean specialist geographical review.
