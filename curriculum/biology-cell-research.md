## Content authoring checks

Use typical-cell language, preserving specification qualifiers **most** and **often**. Do not assert every animal cell contains a nucleus or every plant cell contains chloroplasts: specialised cells can differ, and underground root cells generally lack chloroplasts. A bacterial cell wall is not a cellulose plant wall. Plasmids may be absent or there may be one or more; they are not the main DNA loop and not nuclei. Bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus; saying they have no genetic material is incorrect.

Structure/function explanations (standard GCSE interpretations of the required links): nucleus contains genetic material and controls activities; cytoplasm is the site of many enzyme-controlled reactions; membrane controls exchange; mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration; ribosomes make proteins; chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis; cellulose wall strengthens/supports the cell; permanent vacuole contains cell sap and helps support plant cells. Plasmids carry additional genetic information; optional antibiotic-resistance examples must be explained as possible genes, not universal contents. Do not say mitochondria are the site of *all* respiration, that energy is created, or that a cell wall replaces the cell membrane.

Include a bacterial structure–function retrieval in lesson2 despite its animal/plant heading: the specification explicitly includes plasmids there. Distinguish structure labels from functions and require comparisons rather than only repeated naming.

## Units, magnification and practical calculations

- 1cm=10⁻²m;1mm=10⁻³m;1µm=10⁻⁶m;1nm=10⁻⁹m. Thus1mm=1000µm and1µm=1000nm.
- Magnification=image size/real object size, after expressing both in the same unit. Magnification is a dimensionless factor, conventionally shown ×400 rather than “400mm”. Rearrangements: real size=image size/magnification; image size=magnification×real size.
- Original worked example: a40mm image of a25µm cell has magnification40,000/25=×1600. Fresh check:12mm image at×600 corresponds to.020mm=20µm real size. Do not divide12 by600 and label the resultµm without conversion.
- Supplied scale example: representative eukaryotic cell20µm versus bacterium2µm gives size ratio10,one order of magnitude. Exact fixed sizes are not specified in these paragraphs; present any typical ranges or diagram dimensions as approximate or supplied, not mandatory universal values. Standard form coefficient must lie from1 inclusive to10 exclusive; e.g.20µm=2.0×10⁻⁵m.
- Total optical magnification is eyepiece factor×objective factor: ×10 eyepiece with×4/×10/×40 objectives gives×40/×100/×400. These are handbook examples, not a requirement that every microscope have identical lenses.
- Include relative area as well as length: if two similarly shaped structures have diameters in ratio1:2, projected areas scale1:4. State the shape assumption; an arbitrary outline does not justify squaring a single width. Estimation suits approximate boundaries/irregular shape and initial checks; report sensible precision rather than invented exact organelle areas.
- A rendered scale bar represents real distance and must scale with the picture. Image magnification depends on final displayed/printed size; resizing a screenshot can invalidate a quoted image magnification. Keep calibration with the drawing/image or use a labelled scale bar and supplied measurements.

## RP1 preparation and method boundaries

The specification's RP1 is in4.1.1.2,not numbered under4.1.1.5. It requires observing/drawing/labelling a selection of plant **and animal** cells by light microscopy and including a magnification scale. The handbook uses prepared slides, so onion-peel preparation, cheek sampling and named stains are not compulsory specific methods. Prepared slides are a valid first lesson context and avoid inventing specimen collection requirements.

Handbook sequence: slide on stage; lowest-power objective first; view from the side when bringing lens near slide; look through eyepiece and increase lens–slide distance to bring into focus; use fine focus; locate cells before switching to higher power; refocus with fine adjustment; make clear pencil drawings with labels and record total eyepiece×objective magnification. Adapt to actual microscope controls and teacher guidance. Avoid telling students to drive a lens toward the slide while looking through the eyepiece, or use large coarse movements at high power. School supervision/risk assessment still applies.

Drawing should reflect structures actually observed, not add every textbook organelle regardless of resolution. The handbook asks students to distinguish parts they cannot see from those observed and explain their functions. Observation/interpretation, clean labels and a calibrated scale are meaningful online preparation; actual microscope handling, specimen observation and a contemporaneous biological drawing still require physical practical work. The app must not certify RP1 completion from a simulation or multiple-choice result.

## Visual and model limitations

A colourful 3D cutaway is a **model**, not a light micrograph. Label symbolic colours, enlarged organelles and nonliteral proportions/spacing. Provide text descriptions and a labelled2D view, keyboard controls and no colour-only identification. Clearly distinguish the cell membrane just inside a plant/bacterial wall; a vacuole is inside the membrane, not a hole in the cell.

Resolution is ability to distinguish nearby details; magnification is enlargement. Enlarging a blurred image does not recover lost detail. The official microscopy scope is differences in magnification/resolution and resulting understanding, not mandatory TEM/SEM construction, exact wavelength physics or invented historical dates. A simulated focus/zoom control can illustrate these ideas but should not imply a screen zoom is the same as switching microscope objective or an electron microscope's resolving power.

Ordinary school light microscopy will not resolve ribosomes or reliably reveal all details shown in the model. Mitochondria cannot be promised visible in every ordinary prepared slide; chloroplasts depend on tissue and preparation. Clearly label synthetic images and do not present a decorative cell rendering as a real specimen or measured micrograph. Size comparison needs a common scale or explicit separate scale bars; an equal-sized bacterial and animal drawing must not imply equal real size.
