# AQA GCSE science curriculum plan

Checked against AQA's online specifications on 16 October 2026.

Scope: Combined Science: Trilogy (8464), Biology (8461), Chemistry (8462), Physics (8463). Support Foundation and Higher pathways. Separate science includes additional content within shared topics, as well as additional sections. Higher Tier and separate-science status are independent.

This is a topic and skills inventory for planning. The [full topic index](aqa-topic-index.md) contains 730 numbered headings across all four specifications, including parent headings and recurring content. The accompanying `aqa-spec-index.json` makes that hierarchy and its source URLs available to the app. It is not yet a statement-by-statement lesson coverage audit. Before publishing a lesson, check its full specification paragraphs, including restrictions within paragraphs and inherited from parent sections.

## Proposed first learning sequence

Start with **Atoms, elements, molecules and compounds**. Its anchor is Trilogy **5.1.1.1**, separate Chemistry **4.1.1.1**. Molecules provide a useful introduction, with formal covalent bonding developed later. This is the beginning of chemistry; the biology specification starts with cells, and physics with energy.

1. **Explore:** manipulate a particle scene and notice different particle types.
2. **Distinguish:** identify an atom, an element, a molecule and a compound. Use O2 to show that a molecule need not be a compound.
3. **Build:** assemble H2, O2, H2O and CO2; interpret symbols and count atoms from formulae.
4. **Classify:** sort particle diagrams into elements, compounds and mixtures. Mixtures introduce the next section, 5.1.1.2 / 4.1.1.2.
5. **Explain:** distinguish joining atoms chemically from physically mixing substances. Show that not every compound consists of discrete molecules; revisit ionic lattices later.
6. **Check:** answer unfamiliar diagram/formula questions, receive misconception-specific feedback, retry a different example.
7. **Recall later:** revisit the distinctions through spaced retrieval before advancing into atomic structure and bonding.

Proposed skills: model interpretation (WS1.2), scientific language (WS4.1), visual representation (MS5b). Mark these as lesson-design mappings rather than claiming all are explicitly attached to this AQA paragraph.

## Visual and learning direction

Interpret the requested Cinema 4D aesthetic as polished real-time 3D: softly lit materials, clear depth, controlled camera movement, smooth transitions and restrained interface typography. Use a manipulable scene, not a decorative video. Keep an accurate 2D particle-diagram view alongside the 3D representation to bridge to exam questions.

Every interaction should produce evidence of learning: predict, manipulate, observe, explain, then apply. Show model limitations, including unrealistic scale and symbolic colours/bonds. Provide keyboard controls, labels independent of colour, reduced motion and a lightweight fallback.

Suggested lesson records: qualification/specification references; tier eligibility; prerequisites; learning objectives; misconceptions; interactive task; explanation; WS/MS/AT mappings; practical links; assessment items; mastery evidence; review schedule; source/version. Progress should depend on demonstrated understanding, with targeted remediation and later recall checks.
