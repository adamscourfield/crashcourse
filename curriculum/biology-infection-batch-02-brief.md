## Learning purpose
Explain individual/population vaccination; distinguish antibiotics, antivirals context and symptom relief; explain selection of resistant strains; recall traditional drug origins and modern synthesis; plan preclinical/clinical testing and interpret dose/placebo/peer-review evidence. Prerequisites: human-defences for vaccination; bacterial-diseases/viral-diseases for medicines/resistance; medicines then discovery for trials.

Misconceptions: vaccination treats any existing infection; everyone is guaranteed protected; antibiotics kill viruses; symptom relief proves pathogen removal; antibiotics deliberately cause useful mutations; a natural extract is automatically safe; the largest dose is best; placebo means nobody improves; double blind hides data forever; peer review proves certainty. Wrong predictions/choices receive mechanism-specific explanations and remain retryable.

## Investigate
Vaccination: compare initial/repeat responses, specific matching, and manipulate coverage on a20-node fixed network; predict, step spread and record all3 comparisons. Medicines: compare susceptible bacterial treatment, viral non-response and symptom relief without killing; match antibiotic to supplied susceptibility evidence. Resistance: manually step survival/reproduction with/without selection, calculate proportions and apply stewardship decisions. Discovery: match3 historical origins, build a preclinical sequence and classify toxicity/efficacy/dose evidence using a candidate table. Trials: design blinded/placebo groups, manipulate supplied dose levels and select the lowest level meeting stated benefit/harm criteria, calculate an independent group comparison, interpret peer-review limits.

Completion requires comparisons/data reasoning, not time spent or watching motion. Sources are original schematics, not botanical identification or instruction to self-medicate.
