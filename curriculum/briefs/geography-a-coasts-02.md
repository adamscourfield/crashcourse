## Learning purpose, misconceptions and evidence
| Lesson | Objectives / scope | Diagnostic misconceptions |
|---|---|---|
| deposition |1.4b: explain beach accumulation, spit extension across a change in coast direction, recurved tip, sheltered mud/saltmarsh; a bar may join headlands across a bay, enclosing a lagoon. Connect deposition to sediment supply and transport capacity.|A spit is not every beach; not all spits close an estuary; river flow can keep a mouth open.|
| human-change |1.5a: compare building/roads, agricultural land/drainage, industry/ports/defences; explain erosion loss vs inundation and environmental effects; identify exposed people/assets and unequal consequences.|Flooding is not necessarily permanent land loss; development does not create all erosion; protection can redistribute sediment/risk.|
| hard-defences |1.5b: seawall, groynes and rip rap mechanisms and trade-offs; distinguish wave dissipation/reflection and alongshore sediment trapping; select a context-sensitive judgement.|Groynes do not make sediment; a protected frontage can leave downdrift deficits; no structure guarantees zero risk.|
| soft-management |1.5b: nourishment, managed retreat/realignment, costs and recurring maintenance, land/habitat changes; compare management objectives with uncertainty.|Nourishment is not permanent; managed realignment is deliberate planning, not simply neglect or proof of no losses.|
| swanage |1.6a: locate Swanage Bay/Old Harry in Dorset on England’s south coast; link east-facing discordant coast, Chalk at Ballard/Old Harry, weaker Wealden sands/clays in bay, Purbeck limestones south; connect water attack/weaknesses to stacks and beach/defence management.|White chalk is not un-erodible; one2013 photo cannot establish an erosion rate or current defence condition.|

## Discover and representations
Three causal explanation cards per lesson, including competing outcomes and conditional judgement. Four animated original conceptual process models (deposition, settlement/exposure, hard/soft intervention), selecting four contrasting views each. Default looping playback, Pause, Loop-off, manual0/.5/1 stages; reduced-motion paused. Swanage instead uses static photograph/locator/geology/management evidence controls: time animation would falsely imply measured change in a named place. Original locality diagram clearly not an OS/BGS map and not precise boundaries. Genuine unaltered2013 photo from Wikimedia Commons, Nilfanion, CC BY-SA4.0; attribution/license/source/date visible. Distinguish observation of pale cliffs and detached rock from geological interpretation supported by BGS.

## Investigate
Four challenges per lesson: predict material/risk change before comparing views; identify process from a labelled representation; compare stakeholder/mechanism trade-offs; reject an unsupported generalisation. All labelled views plus all correct challenges required. Mistakes correctable, premature progression blocked. Labels and textual captions support keyboard/non-colour use. Benefits: compares sediment/risk consequences under contrasting choices rather than decorative animation. No quantitative flood model, exact geography, forecast or genuine dated before/after map is implied.
