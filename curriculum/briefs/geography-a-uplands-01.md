## Learning purposes, prerequisites and misconceptions
| Lesson | Observable goals / diagnostic |
|---|---|
| Tees landscape | Locate source/mouth and contrasting reaches; connect High Force geology/inherited meltwater and modern erosion; explain navigation cuts and dated Cow Green flow support; evaluate significance by reach rather than assert one universal control. Preceded by river01/02. Distinguish deliberate navigation cut from natural oxbow and dated operational source from live release data. |
| Moving ice1.11a | Distinguish plucking/abrasion, track debris on/within/beneath ice, explain deposition. Ice particles can move forwards while margin retreats. Preceded by shared past-process/geology teaching. Melting ice does not melt a boulder into water. |
| Upland slopes1.11b | Explain wet-crack repeated freeze–thaw, gradual soil movement/creep, rockfall versus slide and scree. Relict landforms are not inactive. A shared five-second replay is not a real speed comparison. |
| Climate/weather1.11c | Connect past glaciation with inherited forms; distinguish seasonal/diurnal and climate/weather; explain moisture and temperature cycling, rain/thaw/saturation and aspect. Continuous cold is not repeated freeze–thaw. Invented−2/+3°C is a conditional illustration, not UK weather evidence or a failure forecast. |
| Corries/tarns/arêtes1.12a subset | Explain weathering, moving-ice erosion and corrie excavation; water retention after ice loss; sharp bedrock ridge between neighbouring corries. Arête is not deposited sediment; not every corrie contains a lake. Other1.12aforms remain planned. |

Tees: static route and genuine photo; moving particle traces engineered shorter route; finite Cow Green storage/release conceptual illustration. Route comparison not the exact historic channel geometry.2022 source is not live operation or a promise of flood protection.

Ice: downslope particle while margin moves upslope; basal abrasive tools and striation; jointed block detachment/removal; on/within/beneath tracers; released debris stays on ground. Timing and block sizes symbolic; no erosion-rate or sediment-budget claim.

Slopes: crack expansion/thaw, incremental creep, falling/bouncing block with scree and rockslide along slip plane. Rain can affect stability but does not guarantee failure. Creep and rockfall time compression explicitly different.

Climate: changing past ice extent, invented night/day wet-crack crossing, static seasonal concepts, rain/thaw-runoff/slope response. No observed local temperature history fabricated; no fixed claim that all seasons share conditions.

Corrie: rotation cue and deepening basin, static retained tarn and threshold, neighbouring eroding basins with intervening bedrock ridge. Two-dimensional sections simplify the actual three-dimensional glacier and landform; not an OS map. Erosion/deposition remain distinct.
