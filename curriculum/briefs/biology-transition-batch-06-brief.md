## Learning purpose / coverage
Distinguish local disappearance, endangered populations and global extinction; use supplied factors rather than assert single certain causes. Trace pre-existing bacterial variants through antibiotic selection, survival, reproduction and spread; interpret count versus proportion; discuss appropriate prescribing, prescribed-course adherence, agricultural restrictions and slow/costly discovery. Use Linnaean nested ranks/binomial names, Woese domains and microscopy/biochemical evidence to interpret relatedness. Define organism/population/community/ecosystem, plant/animal competition, interdependence and relative stability; interpret direct network perturbations. Explain required abiotic factors from supplied data, not universal optimum assumptions. Detailed adaptations, biotic factors, food chains/cycles/sampling remain planned. Earlier Inheritance audit/specialist gaps remain after roadmap completion.

Prerequisites: existing natural selection/DNA lessons for first3; communities for abiotic. Misconceptions: local loss=global extinction; antibiotics cause purposeful mutation or host resistance; all MRSA untreatable; domain=kingdom; similar appearance alone settles ancestry; stability means zero fluctuation; correlation proves one factor caused change.
