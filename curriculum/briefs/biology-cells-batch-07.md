## Learning purpose / Discover

Explain active transport using gradient and energy; apply root/gut contexts; distinguish passive diffusion/osmosis without assigning a fixed direction relative to a cell. Active has four cards/scenes active:energy,active:root,active:gut,active:energy. Comparison has four transport:diffusion,transport:osmosis,transport:active,transport:diffusion cards.

Root context supplies lower soil ion concentration versus higher cell concentration; large surface helps exchange but cannot itself explain against-gradient transfer. Gut context supplies lower sugar concentration than blood and describes overall absorption without pretending every microscopic membrane step is one literal model pump. Sugars are used in respiration. A stopped model transporter does not make all particles stop or eliminate passive exchange. Passive means no cell-respiration energy requirement, not particles with zero thermal energy.

Worked comparison: equal-volume compartments with relative solute concentrations4 and12, proposed4→12 net transfer, require an energy-linked against-gradient explanation. Diffusion12→4 is passive; osmosis concerns water crossing a partially permeable membrane from dilute to concentrated solution. Direction into a cell alone cannot distinguish them. Models show qualitative conditions, not actual flux measurements or precise ATP budgets.

## Misconceptions / scientific limits

- All inward movement is active → require substance/gradient/energy evidence.
- All water or solute movement is osmosis → osmosis refers to water and a selective barrier.
- No active energy means no molecular motion → passive thermal motion may continue.
- Root area alone reverses gradient → area helps exchange; active mechanism supplies energy dependence.
- Every sugar is always actively absorbed → apply the stated concentrations and overall route.
- Particle counts are measured rates → counts, membrane graphics, arrows and energy status are schematic.
