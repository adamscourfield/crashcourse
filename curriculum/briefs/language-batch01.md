## Learning purpose and sequence
1 Explicit evidence;2 inference;3 vocabulary in context;4 selecting references;5 paraphrase;6 narrator/character distinction;7 sensory detail;8 figurative language;9 verbs and agency;10 syntax and pace;11 opening focus;12 focus shifts;13 withholding information;14 endings;15 structural versus language analysis;16 supported evaluation;17 counter-evidence;18 comparison of possible readings;19 coherent analytical paragraph;20 fresh independent reading response and review.
Each student identifies an accurate textual observation, explains a relevant choice, and qualifies a supported interpretation. Priorities: precision before terminology, local context before effect, fresh writing distinct from selection scores. Misconceptions: plausible guess equals evidence; narrator equals author; short sentence always means tension; device label earns analysis; judgment requires agreement; longer answer means stronger argument.

## Discover
Three original explanations per lesson: secure evidence, work through a particular feature, qualify the resulting claim. Twenty separately labelled original micro-passages give manageable entry points; not historical sources, established-writer extracts, official papers or proof of the whole required text range. Later batches must add lawfully sourced high-quality19th/20th/21st-century fiction/non-fiction. Current passages develop foundations, not full exam endurance.30-minute activity estimates include rereading, discussion and writing; calibrate in pilot.

## Investigate / model decision
Static close reading: complete passage, three selectable details, competing interpretations, ordered reasoning. Guided route reveals details and views at reading pace, then waits for a real answer to an evidence challenge. No decorative3D or motion: text is evidence, not a physical process. One primary Next, persistent pause; keyboard controls/non-colour labels; bounded accessible reading area.
