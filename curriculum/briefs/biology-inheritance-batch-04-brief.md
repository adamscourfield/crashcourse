## Objectives and assessment scope
Explain genetic/environmental/combined phenotype influences; extensive population variation; variants from mutations, usually no phenotype effect, some influence and few determine, continuous mutation and rare new phenotype suited to change. Evolution changes inherited population characteristics across generations through natural selection; shared ancestry from simple early life over3billion years; reproductive isolation definition. Selection acts on existing variants, not need-directed mutation or individuals changing inherited traits by choice. Selective breeding: choose parents and offspring repeatedly over generations; disease-resistant crops, meat/milk, gentle dogs, flowers; reduced diversity/inbreeding risk and ethical trade-offs. Engineering: introduce gene from another organism; crops/disease/fruit/insect/herbicide traits, insulin-producing bacteria, medical research on inherited disorders; benefits, environmental effects and objections evaluated from supplied evidence. Higher enzymes isolate gene, plasmid/virus vector carries it into cells, early development and expression.
