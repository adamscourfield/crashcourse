## Misconceptions

Atoms do not melt individually; bulk material changes arrangement. At melting/boiling points, both phases may coexist. Dissolved is (aq), not (l). Boiling small molecules does not split their covalent bonds. Larger related molecules usually have stronger intermolecular forces; size alone is not a universal cross-substance rule. A polymer is a large molecule, not a giant covalent network; n counts repeat units, not atoms. Silicon dioxide has no discrete SiO₂ molecules in its giant structure. Covalent substances are not universally nonconductors: graphite is an exception taught next.
